    The pattern

    Why every system you've tried quit on you

    Four planners, three weeks each. It was never the layout.

    It needed a setup day you'll never have again

    Setup is novel and fun, so you did it beautifully, once. Maintenance needs the exact faculty you don't have reliable access to. The system went stale, and a to-do list that lies is worse than no list.

    It was built around time, and you can't feel time

    Time blindness isn't a scheduling preference, it's an absent sense. Any system whose main axis is the clock will fail you on a random Tuesday.

    It had no bad-day mode

    Every planner is designed for your best self. But your best self doesn't need a planner. You need something at 2pm on a low day with 31 tabs open.

    It made restarting shameful

    The broken streak, the blank pages. The guilt made the tool radioactive, so you bought a different one, and the cycle started again.

    The honest answers

    I'll just abandon this one too.

    Probably, at least once. That's planned for — it's the only system here designed around abandonment. There's a Re-Entry page: four steps, under five minutes, mostly deleting. No archive purge, no streak to mourn. The difference is that when you come back, it isn't stale, because somebody else was maintaining it.

    Why monthly? Just sell me the template.

    Because a static ADHD template is the exact thing that's already failed you several times. The template was never the product; continuous maintenance of your external executive function is. If you genuinely want a one-time file, don't buy this — you already own four.

    Do I need to know Notion?

    No. You duplicate one link. If you can use Notes, you can use this, and it's built phone-first — most members use it 80% on mobile. The free Notion plan is plenty.

    I'm not diagnosed.

    You don't need a diagnosis to join and nobody will ask. Self-identified, waitlisted, assessed and dismissed, or diagnosed at 38 — same room, same system.

    Is this therapy or coaching?

    No. It's systems and community. No medical advice, no medication discussion, no clinicians. Plenty of members use this alongside therapy or medication; it replaces neither.

    What if I stop using it for a month?

    Then you'll use Re-Entry, which exists precisely for that. Nothing goes stale, because maintaining it isn't your job. And nobody in the community will mention your absence — that's an actual rule.
