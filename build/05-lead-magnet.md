# THE 20-MINUTE RESET

### Read this first (40 seconds)

Everything has piled up. You don't know what's urgent, you half-remember something with a deadline, and the not-knowing is costing more energy than the actual work.

That's not a character flaw. It's what happens when a brain with no reliable working memory and no internal clock accumulates two weeks of inputs.

Here's what we're doing: **20 minutes, five steps, one timer.** At the end you'll have one page that tells you what's real, what's noise, and the single next thing. You are not building a system today. You are not catching up today. You're just getting visibility.

**Set a timer for 20 minutes now.** The timer matters — it's the only thing standing between this and a three-hour reorganising spiral.

One rule: **do not open your email, your old to-do app, or your calendar until Step 3.** Those are rabbit holes and you have 20 minutes.

---

## STEP 1 — The Dump (6 minutes) ⏱

Write down everything taking up space in your head. Not organised. Not prioritised. Not in sentences.

Type or scribble. Voice-memo it if typing is the blocker. Fragments are correct. "mum thing," "that invoice," "the smell in the car" — all valid entries.

**Prompts if you stall (don't read these unless you stall):**
- What have I been avoiding opening?
- Who am I slightly dreading hearing from?
- What did I say I'd do and then not do?
- What's the thing I keep remembering at 1am?
- What physical object in my home is bothering me?
- What's expiring, overdue, or renewing?

**When the timer hits 6 minutes, stop even if you're mid-thought.** More will surface later. More always surfaces later. That's fine.

## STEP 2 — Is anything actually on fire? (3 minutes) ⏱

Go through your list and mark each item with one letter. Three seconds per item, gut call, no deliberating:

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **🔥** | There is a real external consequence in the next 7 days. A fee, a missed appointment, a person genuinely blocked on me. |
| **👤** | Someone is waiting on a reply from me. |
| **—** | Everything else. |

**Be ruthless about 🔥.** "I feel anxious about it" is not a fire. "It renews on Thursday and costs £180" is a fire. Most people mark 6 fires and have 1. Dread is loud; deadlines are quiet.

Now count your fires.

> **If you have zero 🔥:** nothing is on fire. Say that out loud — it's probably the most useful sentence you'll read today. The weight you've been carrying was ambiguity, not urgency.
>
> **If you have one or two:** good. That's the whole picture and it's smaller than it felt.
>
> **If you have more than three:** go back and re-read the definition. Keep only the ones with a date and a consequence. The rest are 👤 or —.

---

## STEP 3 — The 90-second reality check (2 minutes) ⏱

*Now* you may open your calendar and nothing else.

Look at the next 7 days only. Answer these three:

1. **Is there anything in the next 7 days I'd forgotten about?** → add it, mark it 🔥
2. **Is there anything in there I should cancel?** → one appointment, one commitment. There almost always is. Cancelling counts as progress.
3. **Where is my first genuinely empty 30-minute block?** → write the day and time here: `_______________`

Close the calendar. Do not open email.

---

## STEP 4 — Clear the human debt (4 minutes) ⏱

Look at your 👤 items. These are costing you the most per unit of actual work, because unanswered people generate background anxiety continuously.

You're going to send **holding replies**, not real replies. A holding reply takes 15 seconds and buys a week. Copy one of these:

> **Late by days:**
> "Sorry for the slow reply — this is on my list, I'll come back to you by [day]."

> **Late by weeks:**
> "This fell through the cracks on my end, apologies. Still relevant? If so I'll get to it by [day]."

> **You can't actually do it:**
> "I'm not going to be able to take this on — wanted to tell you properly rather than go quiet. Sorry for the delay in saying so."

> **You have no idea what they wanted:**
> "Coming back to this late — can you remind me where we landed? Happy to pick it up."

Send as many as you can in 4 minutes. Don't craft them. Nobody is grading the prose, and "I went quiet for three weeks because I was writing the perfect reply" is the most expensive sentence in ADHD.

**Done is 4 minutes of this, not all of them.**

---

## STEP 5 — One thing, one slot (3 minutes) ⏱

From your 🔥 list, pick the **one** item that will most reduce background noise if it's done.

Not the biggest. Not the most virtuous. The loudest.

```
THE ONE THING: ______________________________________

The first physical action (open what? click what? find what?):
______________________________________

How long I think it'll take: ______ minutes
How long it will actually take (× 2.4): ______ minutes
When: [the empty block from Step 3] ______________
```

That multiplier isn't a joke. Adults with ADHD under-estimate task duration by roughly 2–3× with remarkable consistency. Using the multiplier is the difference between a plan and a fantasy.

**Everything else on your list: leave it.** You don't need to file it, tag it, or move it into an app. It's on the page. The page is the system today.

---

## You're done. Here's what just happened.

In 20 minutes you:
- emptied working memory onto paper, which is where it belongs
- separated real deadlines from dread, which are not the same thing and never were
- found at least one thing to cancel
- cleared human debt that was charging you interest
- got one specific action in one specific slot

**What you did NOT do:** build a system, catch up, or fix your life. On purpose. Those were the goals that broke the last four attempts.

### Do this again when
- the pile feels opaque again (usually 1–3 weeks)
- you come back from being away
- it's 2pm, you've done nothing, and you're spiralling
- a bad week ends

Print it, duplicate it, run it monthly. It works forever and it costs nothing.
