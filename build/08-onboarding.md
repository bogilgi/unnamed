# 08 — Member Onboarding Sequence (emails + Discord)

## Design principles

1. **The first session must end in one captured thought and one chosen task.** Nothing else. Every additional setup step measurably increases day-1 abandonment.
2. **Explicitly forbid full setup on day one.** Counter-intuitive, and it's the highest-leverage line in the whole sequence. New ADHD members will otherwise spend four hours configuring and never return — the exact failure mode that killed their last four systems.
3. **One CTA per message.** Always one. Never two.
4. **Normalise disappearing before it happens.** Mention Re-Entry in week one, while they're still enthusiastic, so it's already permitted when they need it in week five.
5. **Emails are short.** Under 120 words wherever possible. Long onboarding emails don't get read by this audience; that's not a flaw to write around, it's a constraint to design for.

## DISCORD SEQUENCE

### Pinned in `#welcome-and-rules`

> ## Welcome to Dopamine Desk 👋
>
> **Three rules. They're the whole culture.**
>
> **1. No productivity shame.** Not at others, not at yourself. "I've achieved nothing in three weeks" is fine to say; "I'm so lazy, sorry for the whinge" gets gently pushed back on. We don't do self-flagellation here, even about yourself.
>
> **2. No "just try harder."** No "have you tried a planner." No neurotypical advice unless it was asked for. If you're not sure whether a suggestion is helpful, ask first: *"want suggestions or want to vent?"*
>
> **3. No medical advice.** No medication recommendations, doses, sourcing, or diagnosis opinions — not from me, not from members. Share your own experience freely; don't prescribe. We're not clinicians and getting this wrong can hurt someone.
>
> **Also true:**
> - Nobody comments on your absence. Ever. You can vanish for three months and reappear with no explanation.
> - Lurking is full participation. Read-only is a valid way to be a member.
> - Low-effort messages are welcome. "bad day, nothing to say, hi" is a complete and good post.
> - Venting ≠ asking for solutions. Say which one you want.
> - No DM pitching, no self-promo without asking, no recruiting.
>
> **Where to go**
> `#introduce-yourself` — three sentences max, no pressure
> `#wins-and-progress` — including tiny ones. Especially tiny ones.
> `#system-help` — anything about your Desk
> `#accountability` — check-ins, pairing
> `#body-doubling` — Open Desk schedule + voice rooms
> `#low-energy-days` — our most-used channel. Low-stakes, no advice unless asked.
> `#resources-and-shares` · `#off-topic`
>
> **Start here:** post one line in `#introduce-yourself` and one thing in `#wins-and-progress`, however small. Or don't — reading is fine too.

### Pinned in `#low-energy-days`

> This channel is for days when there isn't much to report.
>
> You can post a single emoji. You can post "existing." You can post nothing and just read.
>
> **The one rule here:** no advice unless someone explicitly asks. Hearts, "same," and "that sounds hard" are the whole toolkit. If someone's struggling and you've got The Perfect Tip, sit on it — or ask first.

### Pinned in `#wins-and-progress`

> Post wins here. We mean the small ones.
>
> Answered an email. Took your meds four days running. Deleted eleven dead tasks. Showed up to one Open Desk. Opened the Desk at all after two weeks away.
>
> If it felt hard and you did it, it's a win. We don't rank them and we don't compare them.

### Day 2 DM (manual, from you personally — do this while you can)

> Hey — [Name] here, I run this. Genuinely just saying hi, no sales thing.
>
> Two questions if you have the energy, one sentence each is perfect:
> 1. What made you finally sign up?
> 2. What's the one thing you're hoping this fixes?
>
> Answer 2 goes straight into the roadmap. And if you don't feel like replying, that's completely fine — no follow-up.

> **Do this manually for your first 100 members.** It is the highest-ROI activity in the entire business: it is your retention mechanism, your market research, and your testimonial pipeline at once. Automate it only when it becomes physically impossible.
