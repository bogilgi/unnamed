## THE PROBLEM SECTION

### Why every system you've tried quit on you

**1. It required a setup day you'll never have again.**
Normal systems assume a one-time 4-hour configuration, and then maintenance you'll "just do weekly." You did it once. Beautifully. Then you never touched the maintenance, and the system quietly went stale until it was lying to you. A to-do list that lies is worse than no list.

**2. It was built around time, and you can't feel time.**
"Block 9–11am for deep work." Cool. You didn't know it was 11am. Time blindness isn't a scheduling preference, it's an absent sense. Any system whose primary axis is the clock will fail you on a random Tuesday.

**3. It had no bad-day mode.**
Every planner on earth is designed for your best self. But you don't need a system on good days — on good days you're unstoppable. You need a system at 2pm on a low day when your meds have worn off and there are 31 open tabs. Nothing you bought has a 2pm-low-day screen.

**4. It made restarting shameful.**
The half-filled habit tracker. The 400-day streak you broke on day 61. The guilt made the tool radioactive, so you bought a different one, and the cycle started again. Average ADHD adult: 4–7 abandoned systems. You're not broken, you're a repeat customer of an industry that keeps selling you the same shape of failure.

> **The fix isn't more willpower, more features or a prettier template. It's a system someone else keeps alive, that assumes you'll leave and come back, and that is sorted by energy instead of by time.**
